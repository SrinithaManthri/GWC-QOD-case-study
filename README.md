# GWC-QOD-case-study
Healthcare decision automation case study comparing Rule-Based and Data-Driven AI systems for Girls Who Code.
# QoD System Recommendation

**Project:** Healthcare Decision Automation Analysis  
**Program:** Girls Who Code  

---

## Executive Summary

This case study evaluates whether a **Rule-Based** or **Data-Driven** system is better suited for automating Quality of Decision (QoD) choices in healthcare. Based on our decision table analysis, a **Data-Driven approach** is recommended to handle complex, ambiguous clinical scenarios and learn from past medical outcomes.

---

## Reasoning

Healthcare QoD decisions depend on multiple interacting factors. A data-driven system can learn complex relationships from historical cases, making it far better equipped to optimize decisions in uncertain, high-stakes clinical situations.

---

## Evidence

Our QoD decision table contained an ambiguous scenario marked `'Maybe Yes/No'`. The presence of this edge case proved that static, predefined rules cannot cover every real-world healthcare situation.

---

## Why Data-Driven Over Rule-Based?

While rule-based systems offer strong transparency and ease of explanation, fixed rules fall short when encountering unscripted clinical scenarios. A data-driven approach better navigates real-world uncertainty by analyzing patterns in past data.

---

## Tradeoffs & Conclusion

Although rule-based architecture excels in governance and explainability, a **Data-Driven System** provides the flexibility necessary to manage complex, ambiguous QoD cases safely. Implementing model explainability tools (such as feature attribution) can help mitigate the reduced explainability tradeoff of data-driven models.
